RiftLinux is a modified Monado, specialized for full Oculus Rift CV1 support: the headset, its Constellation sensors
and its Touch controllers on Linux, in SteamVR and in OpenXR applications, from one package. No Windows, no Oculus
software, no Meta account.

This release makes the headset's tracking steady where 0.1.0 could jitter or jump, keeps a headset no sensor sees
where it was, and fixes how RiftLinux behaves with a headset whose wear sensor does not notice the face.

## Fixed

- **Steady tracking with the rear headband's LEDs.** Where the rear headband's LEDs sit is measured on your headset
  and saved; the strap sits a little differently each time the headset is put on. A saved measurement is now checked
  against the headset's front LEDs before it is used, measured again when it no longer fits, and followed when the
  strap moves. Before, a measurement from another time bent the headset's position, and the view shook and jumped as
  you turned.
- **Sensors stay where they stand.** Where the sensors stand is learned from the headset's front LEDs only, so the rear
  headband can no longer move a sensor's placement (the two sensors then disagreed on where the headset was).
- **No sensor notices from a headset put down.** With the headset lying still in view of two sensors, on a bed or a
  desk, a sensor could be taken for moved and placed again in the wrong spot; the next start then showed sensor
  notices while it was put back. Only views of a headset that moves around are compared now, and a sensor that
  really moved is still found and placed again.
- **The headset out of every sensor's view.** The view slid away and came back with a jump. Now the headset stays
  where it was and turns about your neck, following its own gyro, as the Oculus runtime holds a headset it cannot see,
  and goes straight back to where the sensors see it. It is found again as soon as a sensor sees it, also after a
  long time out of view and when it was turned in the hands meanwhile; before, it could stay lost for a while.
- **Steady with one sensor.** When only one sensor saw the headset, its position could drift towards or away from
  that sensor and then jump back. The headset's motion sensor is now weighed less against what the sensor sees, and
  the view stays where the sensor sees the headset.
- **Tracked with your back to the sensors.** The rear headband's LEDs are measured from a few turns while the headset
  is worn, also with a wear sensor that misses the face, and the headset stays tracked when you turn around.
- **Smoother corrections.** A correction of the headset's or a controller's position is blended in rather than jumped
  to, also when a second sensor's view of the same moment comes in.
- **A controller seen again.** Right after it was found, a controller could be shown far from where it was; it is now
  shown where the sensors saw it.
- **Thumbsticks.** A Touch thumbstick resting just off its centre read as pushed, mostly forward, and games that
  walk you where you look kept walking you on; a stick at rest now reads zero. A thumb on the stick was often not
  seen, and older games that move you only while it is touched stopped moving you; a pushed stick now always
  counts as touched.
- **A wear sensor that misses the face.** The display went dark while the headset was worn, and SteamVR went into
  standby. Now the headset also counts as worn while it moves as a head does, so the display stays on and SteamVR
  runs; put down, it goes dark as before, also when it is bumped on a desk or lies on a bed. The sensor also takes a
  face held a little further away as worn, as with a thicker face pad.
- **Facing the right way after putting the headset on.** The world was sometimes placed on you while the headset
  was still being put on, from a moment it was held still in the hands, and faced the wrong way. It is placed once
  the headset has been worn for a moment and you look steadily ahead.
- **SteamVR Home.** SteamVR 2.18 starts without SteamVR Home unless it is switched on: RiftLinux switches it on when
  the headset is set up, unless you switched it off yourself.
- **A clean log.** The warnings every start wrote (a wireless receiver's extra device, a sensor's first half picture,
  its radio's replies, a Vulkan extension listed twice, the display going dark) are gone; real-time priority now also
  takes when SteamVR or the runtime is started from a terminal over SSH or under a scheduler that lowers it; a sensor
  whose pictures stop is reported, with what still arrived.
- The repository's licence information covers every file again.

## Tested on

- NVIDIA graphics, drivers 580 and 615; Ubuntu 25.04 (GNOME, X11 and Wayland) and CachyOS (KDE Plasma, Wayland);
  two sensors, and three for room scale.
- The packages are installed and checked in clean Ubuntu 22.04, 24.04 and 26.04, Debian 12 and 13, Fedora 43 and 44,
  and Arch systems.
- AMD and Intel graphics should work (Vulkan), but are not tested yet: reports are very welcome.
- x86-64 computers only.

## Install

Download the package for your distribution and `SHA256SUMS` below, check them with
`sha256sum --ignore-missing -c SHA256SUMS`, and install (an upgrade from 0.1.0 is the same command):

```bash
sudo apt install ./riftlinux_0.1.1_amd64.deb                  # Debian, Ubuntu, Linux Mint, Pop!_OS
sudo pacman -U riftlinux-0.1.1-1-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst           # Arch, CachyOS, EndeavourOS, Manjaro
sudo dnf install ./riftlinux-0.1.1-1.x86_64.rpm               # Fedora
tar xf riftlinux-0.1.1-x86_64.tar.gz && sudo ./riftlinux-0.1.1-x86_64/install.sh   # any other distribution
```

Then plug in the Rift and start SteamVR from Steam. The
[user guide](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftLinux/blob/main/docs/README.md) walks through the first start; the README has
the settings and the comparison with other options.

## Known issues

- Steam's client can crash when a flat (non-VR) game opens a window while SteamVR runs: a Valve bug, not this driver
  ([SteamVR for Linux #963](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/SteamVR-for-Linux/issues/963): the same crash, reported for the Desktop tab).
- SteamVR's compositor can crash as SteamVR quits, after the session has ended: harmless, and SteamVR's own exit code
  ([SteamVR for Linux #990](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/SteamVR-for-Linux/issues/990): the same crash with Valve's own streaming driver).
- If SteamVR's compositor stops while SteamVR runs (seen once, after a GPU fault on NVIDIA right after SteamVR left
  standby), SteamVR does not start it again and no game starts: close SteamVR and start it again.
- Steam asks some questions before a game's first start on the monitor, not in the headset: if a game does not
  start from inside VR, take the headset off and answer them there.
- SteamVR's Desktop tab is empty on Linux ([SteamVR for Linux #693](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/SteamVR-for-Linux/issues/693)); WayVR shows the desktop as an overlay. In RiftLinux's own runtime WayVR's
  panels look softer than games, and not every controller button does what WayVR expects (being looked into).
- With NVIDIA on Wayland and a VRR monitor, the headset's picture can tear unless VRR is hidden from the driver
  (NVIDIA's bug, [open-gpu-kernel-modules #992](https://github.com/NVIDIA/open-gpu-kernel-modules/issues/992)): the README's Wayland section has the setting.
- GNOME with HDR on can leave the desktop's colours washed out when the headset connects or disconnects; switching
  HDR off and on in GNOME's settings restores them (likely [mutter #5083](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/mutter/-/work_items/5083), fixed for GNOME 51).
- Pairing the Oculus Remote is not tested yet.

The README's Known issues section has the full list, including app issues under Proton.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue with the bug form and add the output of `riftlinux-setup --json check` and SteamVR's log
(`~/.local/share/Steam/logs/vrserver.txt`); say which GPU and driver you have.

For a tracking problem, record a session: run `riftlinux-setup record on`, start SteamVR and do what shows the
problem, then attach the recording from `~/.cache/riftlinux` and SteamVR's startup log
(`~/.steam/steam/logs/vrstartup-linux.txt`). Turn it off afterwards with `riftlinux-setup record off`.
